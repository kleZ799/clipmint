# How short-form video is distributed: the growth playbook

This is what the app's packaging writer works from when it writes titles,
descriptions, captions, hashtags and tags. It is written for that writer, so it
says what each platform measures and what the packaging should do about it.

**Keeping it current.** When a platform changes how it ranks short videos,
edit this file, raise the `reviewed:` date on the first line and push to
`main`. Every running copy of the app picks up a newer version from the
repository within a day, without waiting for a release. If you keep your own
copy at `playbook.md` in the app's settings folder, the app uses yours instead.

Where this file and a platform's own current guidance disagree, the platform's
guidance wins.

---

## What every platform has in common

- **Distribution runs in rounds.** A new video is shown to a small test
  audience first. How that audience responds decides whether it reaches a
  bigger one. Packaging exists to win that first round with strangers, not
  with existing fans.
- **The first second decides everything else.** Scrolling past is the
  strongest negative signal on every feed. The opening frame, the first spoken
  line and the on-screen text all have to give a stranger a reason to stay.
- **Watching it all and watching again beat everything.** Completion and
  replays tell the system that a video delivered. A title or caption that
  promises more than the clip delivers loses viewers early, and that costs more
  reach than a plain title would.
- **Sharing is the strongest form of engagement.** A send or share puts the
  video in front of someone who was never going to see it. Write the kind of
  line people forward to a friend: specific, funny, surprising or useful.
- **Search is a second life.** All three platforms index captions, titles,
  on-screen text and spoken words for search. A video packaged around a phrase
  people type keeps getting found long after the feed stops pushing it.
- **Original beats reposted.** Every platform limits distribution of content
  reposted without adding anything, and of videos carrying another app's
  watermark. Clips cut from the creator's own long video are original. Post a
  clean file to each platform, never one downloaded from another app.
- **Write for a stranger.** The test audience has never heard of the creator.
  Nicknames, running jokes and "he did it again" framing only work on people
  who are already fans.

## YouTube Shorts

**What it measures**
- Whether people who were shown the Short chose to watch it or swiped away
  (YouTube Studio reports this as "viewed vs. swiped away").
- How much of it they watched, and whether they watched it again. Every play
  and replay counts as a view, so a clip that loops cleanly earns views twice.
- Likes, comments, shares, subscribes after watching, and "not interested".
- A Short can find its audience days or weeks after posting. Early numbers
  are not the final verdict.

**Packaging**
- **Title.** It shows over the video in the feed and is read in under a
  second, and it matters for Shorts search and suggestions. Put the payoff in
  the first 40 characters and name the subject when it is confirmed. Aim for
  35-70 characters, no hashtags in the title, no ALL CAPS, no filler like
  "wait for it".
- **Description.** The first line must hold the main search phrase as a full
  sentence. Lines 2-3 say plainly what happens, with real nouns, because this
  text tells YouTube who to show the clip to. End with a specific question
  about this clip rather than "comment below".
- **Hashtags.** 3-5 in the description, the most specific first. `#shorts` is
  optional, since vertical videos up to three minutes are Shorts anyway, but
  it costs nothing. YouTube ignores every hashtag on a video that has more
  than 60.
- **Tags.** A minor signal. Their main use is spellings people get wrong and
  other names for the subject. Rank them: exact subject, real search phrases,
  variants, genre, kind of moment, format.
- **On-screen text.** A caption in the first two seconds that raises a
  question the next three seconds answer. It must not repeat the title.
- **Length.** Shorts can run up to three minutes, but a clip finishes more
  often when it is only as long as the moment. 15-35 seconds suits most
  moments.
- **Related video.** Link the full video as the Short's related video, so
  viewers who liked the clip have somewhere to go.

**What gets held back**
- Titles the clip does not deliver, and engagement bait.
- Reposted content with nothing added. Channels posting mass-produced or
  repetitive content can lose monetisation.

## Instagram Reels

**What it measures**
- Instagram has named three signals as the most important: **watch time**,
  **likes per reach** and **sends per reach**. Sends (shared by DM) matter
  most for reaching people who don't follow the account, and likes matter
  more with followers.
- Saves and comments count too. A save means someone found it useful, so
  practical clips should say something worth coming back to.
- Reels up to three minutes can be recommended to people who don't follow the
  account.
- Instagram favours original content, and reduces recommendations for
  accounts that mostly repost other people's videos or post videos with
  another app's watermark.

**Packaging**
- **Caption.** Only about the first 125 characters show before "more", so the
  hook and the main keyword go there. Instagram search reads caption keywords,
  and public posts from professional accounts can appear in Google results,
  so write the words someone would search, in a normal sentence. Then one or
  two short lines of context and a call to action that asks for a send or a
  save when the clip suits it ("send this to the friend who..."). Captions can
  run to 2,200 characters, but shorter reads better.
- **Hashtags.** 3-5, specific to the clip: the subject, the niche, the kind of
  moment. Hashtags are a weak signal on Instagram now and a long block reads
  as spam. Never `#shorts`, `#ytshorts` or `#fyp`.
- **Cover text.** The profile grid shows a 3:4 crop of the cover, so a short
  title of 2-5 words must sit in the middle of the frame.
- **Alt text.** A plain sentence describing what happens in the clip. It helps
  accessibility and search.
- **On-screen text.** Many people watch Reels muted, so the hook has to read
  as text, not only as audio.

**What gets held back**
- Reposts and other apps' watermarks, low-resolution uploads, and
  engagement bait ("like if you agree").

## TikTok

**What it measures**
- Completion, rewatches, shares, comments, follows from the video, and "not
  interested". Every video is tested on a small audience, and follower count
  matters less than how that audience responds.
- TikTok is used as a search engine. Keywords in the caption, on-screen text
  and spoken words all count toward being found.

**Packaging**
- **Caption.** Put the search phrase in the first line, in natural words. Add a
  line of context and end with a question that invites comments. Captions can
  be long, but the first line carries it.
- **Hashtags.** 3-5, mixing one broad community tag with specific ones about
  the subject and the moment. `#fyp` and `#foryou` do nothing reliable, so
  don't spend a slot on them. Never `#shorts`.
- **On-screen text.** A hook in the first second, and keywords on screen for
  search.

**What gets held back**
- Reuploads with nothing added, other apps' watermarks, and misleading
  captions.
