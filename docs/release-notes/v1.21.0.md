ClipMint now reads a stream's chat, and ranks on what the live audience
actually reacted to.

Every other thing the ranking knows about a moment is a guess. The AI guesses
from the words, the loudness check guesses from the volume, the vision check
guesses from four frames. A live chat is not a guess. It is the people who were
watching, typing "WHAT" at the same second.

## It reads the chat

- **Fetched while the video transcribes.** On a YouTube stream that kept its
  chat replay, ClipMint downloads it alongside the transcription, so it costs
  no extra waiting on most runs. It's kept beside the video as a tiny summary,
  so a second run over the same stream doesn't fetch it again.
- **It finds the moments the words miss.** The biggest bursts of chat are
  marked in the transcript the AI ranks from, so it now finds moments nobody
  said anything about: a clutch played in silence, a jump scare answered with
  a gasp, something on screen chat saw before you did.
- **Every candidate is scored on how hard chat reacted.** Each spike is
  measured against how busy chat usually is at that point in the stream, so a
  packed finale doesn't drown out a big moment early on. Hellos, goodbyes and
  "just subbed" floods don't count.
- **It knows when you asked for it.** When you say *"everybody type W"* or
  *"type 1 if you agree"*, the flood that follows is your request, not a
  moment. On the stream this was tested on, half of the biggest bursts were
  exactly that, so ClipMint listens for you asking and sets those aside.
- **A new Chat bar** on the clip card, next to Hook, Moment, Look, Energy and
  Pace, and a note, *"Chat went off"*, when the live audience reacted.

## When there's no chat

Nothing changes. A video that isn't a stream, a stream whose channel turned
the chat replay off, or a chat too quiet to mean anything (under about 150
messages, or 3 a minute) is ranked exactly as before, and nothing already
ranked is thrown away. Small streams usually fall here: a "spike" in a chat of
a dozen people is three of them saying hello at once, and ClipMint would
rather not pretend.

Only YouTube chat replays are read for now. A Twitch or Kick VOD is ranked
without its chat.

**Downloading a VOD yourself?** Save its chat next to it with
`yt-dlp --write-subs --sub-langs live_chat <url>` and ClipMint will read the
`.live_chat.json` file beside the video.

---

**ClipMint.exe**: about 224 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**ClipMint-linux-x86_64**: about 280 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc 2.35 or
newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop distribution.**
Every published build is started by the release workflow and asked for its
interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet made a Short with it
on Fedora or Ubuntu. Take `ClipMint-linux-README.txt` too.

**ClipMint-macOS-arm64.zip**: about 167 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still a beta,
and still has never been run on a Mac.** I don't own one, so it is built and
checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a person opening it.
Uploading to YouTube has only been tested on Windows. If it doesn't start, or
signing in doesn't behave as described, that is a bug here:
`~/Movies/ClipMint/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/clipmint/issues/new) are how it gets
fixed.

Also published, for installs from before the rename only: `StreamToShorts.exe`,
`StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64` and `StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip`, the same
three files under their old names. A fresh download wants the ClipMint ones.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
