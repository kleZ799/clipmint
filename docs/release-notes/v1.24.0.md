ClipMint now reads how the Shorts you posted actually did, tells you what's
working on your channel, and learns from it, carefully.

Until now the ranking was a guess that was never checked. Now it gets checked.

## How your Shorts did

Connect your channel in Settings, and the library opens with a new panel:

- **Every clip on your channel is found,** including ones you uploaded
  yourself, which ClipMint matches by title. Each card shows its views, and
  **Most views** sorts a run by them.
- **It says what's working, in plain words.** For example: *"Clips with a
  title the AI actually wrote got a median of 42.5 views; clips without got
  3.5 (10x more)."*
- **Every pattern is tested against chance.** With a few dozen Shorts most
  differences are noise, so a finding is marked **Looks real** only when it
  clearly isn't. Everything else is listed as *no clear link yet*, rather than
  dressed up as advice.
- **Only clips at least two days old are compared.** Yesterday's Short hasn't
  had its views yet.

## The ranking learns

When one of the measured signals (loudness, reaction words, motion, build-up or
chat) really does go with views on your channel, it counts for more in your
next run. It only moves after a dozen clips, never by more than 40%, and only on
a finding that held up. Until then the ranking stays exactly as it was, and the
panel says so.

On the channel ClipMint was built with, that's what happened: none of the
ranking scores tracked views yet, so nothing moved. The one clear finding was
the titles, and it was 10×. If an AI provider runs out of quota before titles
are written, a free Groq key in Settings is the fix.

## Your data

It uses the same YouTube connection as uploading. There's no new permission to
grant. ClipMint checks by itself at most every six hours, or when you press
**Check now**. The numbers stay on your PC, are deleted after 30 days without a
refresh (YouTube's rule), and go the moment you disconnect. The
[privacy policy](https://klez799.github.io/clipmint/privacy.html) has the
details.

---

**ClipMint.exe**: about 224 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**ClipMint-linux-x86_64**: about 280 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc 2.35 or
newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop distribution.**
Every published build is started by the release workflow and asked for its
interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet made a Short with it
on Fedora or Ubuntu. Take `ClipMint-linux-README.txt` too.

**ClipMint-macOS-arm64.zip**: about 167 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still a beta,
and still has never been run on a Mac.** I don't own one, so it is built and
checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a person opening it.
Uploading to YouTube has only been tested on Windows. If it doesn't start, or
signing in doesn't behave as described, that is a bug here:
`~/Movies/ClipMint/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/clipmint/issues/new) are how it gets
fixed.

Also published, for installs from before the rename only: `StreamToShorts.exe`,
`StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64` and `StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip`, the same
three files under their old names. A fresh download wants the ClipMint ones.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
