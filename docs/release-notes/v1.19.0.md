Every clip now comes back edited: captions burned in, dead air cut, a zoom on
the line that matters. And every clip tells you why it ranked where it did.

## Captions, burned in

Most Shorts are watched on mute. Every clip now has captions on the video
itself, a few words at a time, with the word being said lit up as it is said.

- **Four looks:** Bold, Punch, Clean and Comic. Pick one under **Render**, or
  say it in the prompt: *"comic captions"*.
- **The fonts ship with the app**, so a caption looks the same on every PC.
- **On the webcam-over-gameplay layout** the captions sit on the seam between
  the two panels, where they cover neither your face nor the game.
- **Whisper got a name wrong?** Open the clip, press **Captions**, type the line
  as it should read and burn it in again.

## The edit, done for you

- **Dead air goes.** Quiet pauses and "um"s are cut. A pause with sound in it
  stays: a laugh, the game going off while you are silent, the quiet beat
  right before the clip's loudest moment.
- **Punch-ins** zoom in on the lines said with emphasis.
- **Emoji** (off by default) pop on words like "insane", "money" or "no way".
- **B-roll** (off by default) puts two or three seconds of stock footage over
  lines that name something you can film. It needs a free Pexels key in
  Settings, and never runs on a stream.
- **Your logo**, uploaded once in Settings, goes in the corner of every clip.

Each clip is listened to again for word timings, so a run takes longer: about
the render time again on a CPU, very little on an NVIDIA GPU. Everything here
can be switched off under **Render**, or in words: *"keep the pauses, no
captions"*. Clips made before this version keep their original look when you
trim them.

## Why a clip ranked where it did

Every clip card now splits its score into four bars (**Hook**, **Moment**,
**Energy**, **Pace**) with a sentence on why the moment works. Open **Boost**
for the numbers and a grade. The library can sort a run by its strongest hook,
its most energy, or its shortest clip.

**ClipMint.exe**: about 220 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**ClipMint-linux-x86_64**: about 278 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc 2.35 or
newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop distribution.**
Every published build is started by the release workflow and asked for its
interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet made a Short with it
on Fedora or Ubuntu. Take `ClipMint-linux-README.txt` too.

**ClipMint-macOS-arm64.zip**: about 165 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still a beta,
and still has never been run on a Mac.** I don't own one, so it is built and
checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a person opening it.
Uploading to YouTube has only been tested on Windows. If it doesn't start, or
signing in doesn't behave as described, that is a bug here:
`~/Movies/ClipMint/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/clipmint/issues/new) are how it gets
fixed.

Also published, for installs from before the rename only: `StreamToShorts.exe`,
`StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64` and `StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip`, the same
three files under their old names. A fresh download wants the ClipMint ones.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
