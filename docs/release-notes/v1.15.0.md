Stream to Shorts is now **ClipMint**. Same app, same pipeline, same licence —
it has a name that is its own. Your key and your clips come with it.

And the download that looked like it would take all day never actually was
taking all day. That is fixed too, further down.

## What actually changes for you

The window says ClipMint, the download is called `ClipMint.exe`, and the
repository lives at `github.com/kleZ799/clipmint`. How it finds moments and
cuts them is untouched — the only working change in this release is to the
download, below.

**Your API key and your settings come with you.** The first launch copies them
out of the old `StreamToShorts` folder into the new `ClipMint` one, so there is
nothing to re-enter. The copy is left where it was, untouched, in case you go
back to an older build.

**Your clips come with you too.** `Videos\StreamToShorts` is renamed to
`Videos\ClipMint` — the folder is moved, not duplicated, so nothing is
re-downloaded and no disk space is used twice. On a Mac that is `~/Movies`.

If you had already made clips under the new name, neither of those runs; what
is there is yours and is left alone.

## Updating from an older version

It works exactly as it always has. This release also publishes copies of every
download under its old `StreamToShorts` name, because that is the filename your
installed copy asks for — without them, an app on v1.14.1 or earlier would
simply never see this release.

One wrinkle on Windows and Linux: the update replaces the file you launched, in
place, so it keeps the name it already had. You will be running ClipMint out of
a file still called `StreamToShorts.exe`. Rename it whenever you like, or take
the fresh `ClipMint.exe` below — either is the same build.

The old names go away in a later release, so this is the version to take.

## The download was not as slow as it said it was

If you have watched a long stream sit at *"Fetching the video 0% at 126KB/s,
21h46m left"* and given up on it — that number was wrong, and it has been
wrong since downloads started reporting a speed at all.

It was the very first speed reading of the transfer, taken before the
connection had got going. It comes out at around a hundred KB/s no matter how
fast your internet is, and on a multi-gigabyte VOD it turns into an estimate
of most of a day. The download itself was usually running at full speed the
whole time. The giveaway was a 25 MB file that announced *"1m11s left"* and
then finished in three seconds.

Now the speed is measured over the last twenty seconds, and nothing is shown
until there is enough to go on. The first second or two says just *"Fetching
the video 0%"*, and then a figure that holds steady and an estimate that
counts down properly. On a four-hour stream that reads 36MB/s and about three
minutes, where it used to read 126KB/s and most of a day.

Separately, the video is now fetched in 10 MB pieces rather than one long
connection. YouTube slows a single connection the longer it stays open, and
asking in pieces avoids it. On a healthy connection this changes nothing you
would notice — it is there for the ones where the download really does crawl.

---

**ClipMint.exe** — about 220 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**ClipMint-linux-x86_64** — about 278 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc
2.35 or newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop
distribution** — every published build is started by the release workflow and
asked for its interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet sat at
Fedora and made a Short with it. Take `ClipMint-linux-README.txt` too.

**ClipMint-macOS-arm64.zip** — about 165 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still
a beta, and still has never been run on a Mac** — I do not own one, so it is
built and checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a human
opening it. If it does not start, that is a bug here:
`~/Movies/ClipMint/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/clipmint/issues/new) are how it
gets fixed.

Also published, for older installs only: `StreamToShorts.exe`,
`StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64` and `StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip`. They are
byte-for-byte the three files above. A fresh download wants the ClipMint ones.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
