Stream to Shorts is now an all-in-one short-form generator. Give it a stream, a
vlog, a podcast or a tutorial, and it packages every clip for YouTube Shorts,
Instagram Reels and TikTok.

## It knows what kind of video it is looking at

A stream, a vlog, a podcast and a tutorial are good for different reasons, and
until now the app ranked every video as if it were a game stream, which is why
vlog and podcast clips could come out strange. Now it works out which one it is,
from the start, middle and end of what is said plus the video's title and
description, and ranks moments by that kind's own rules:

- **Stream or gaming:** your reaction to the game, never a stretch of game
  narration without you.
- **Vlog:** the payoff of what happened, a plan falling apart, a first time.
- **Podcast or interview:** the hot take, the confession, the argument while
  it is still heated.
- **Tutorial:** one complete tip, or a common mistake and its fix.

Or tell it yourself. **Kind of video** sits under the prompt, next to Shape, or
you can write *"my vlog"* or *"podcast"* in the prompt. Picking Vlog or Podcast
switches the preview to a crop that follows the face, unless you already asked
for a different framing.

## Boost now packages for three apps

Open **Boost** on any clip and there is a tab for each app:

- **YouTube Shorts:** five ranked titles as before, now with **two ranked
  descriptions** to choose between and a score on every tag.
- **Instagram Reels:** **two ranked captions**, one written for search and one
  written to be sent to a friend, with the hook inside the first 125 characters
  where Instagram cuts to "more". Plus hashtags, **cover text** for the grid, and
  **alt text**.
- **TikTok:** a caption built around what people search, and hashtags without
  `#fyp` filler.

**Copy everything** copies for whichever tab is open, and **Save changes** keeps
your wording on all three.

Clips made before this update have no Reels or TikTok captions yet. Open Boost
and hit **Rewrite** to write them.

## Written from a playbook that keeps up

The titles and captions are now written by an AI working as a social media
strategist, from a **growth playbook**: what YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels and
TikTok measure right now, and how to package for it. When a platform changes how
it ranks videos, the playbook gets updated in the repository, and **the app picks
up the new version by itself within a day, without a new download**. If you'd
rather use your own notes, put a `playbook.md` in the app's settings folder.

## Worth knowing

- Writing Instagram and TikTok captions is **one more AI request per run**.
  If that request fails, the YouTube text is reused for them with `#shorts`
  removed, and Boost says so.
- The scores are useful for **ordering** the options. The numbers themselves sit
  high, so a 97 next to a 93 is close to a tie.
- A run that was interrupted before this update ranks its video again when you
  resume it, because the way moments are chosen has changed.

---

**StreamToShorts.exe** — about 220 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64** — about 278 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc
2.35 or newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop
distribution** — every published build is started by the release workflow and
asked for its interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet sat at
Fedora and made a Short with it. Take `StreamToShorts-linux-README.txt` too.

**StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip** — about 165 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still
a beta, and still has never been run on a Mac** — I do not own one, so it is
built and checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a human
opening it. If it does not start, that is a bug here:
`~/Movies/StreamToShorts/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/stream-to-shorts/issues/new) are how it
gets fixed.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
