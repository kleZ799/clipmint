Your clips can go straight to your YouTube channel from the app, now or on a
schedule, with the title, description and tags already filled in.

## Post it to your channel

**Settings → Post to your channel → Connect YouTube.** Your browser opens
Google's own sign-in page. Pick your channel and allow the two permissions, and
you're connected. You only do this once.

Then open any clip, press **Boost**, and scroll the **YouTube Shorts** tab to
**Post it to your channel**:

- **Who can see it:** Public, Unlisted, Private, or **Scheduled** for a time
  you pick
- **Category**, and YouTube's **Made for kids** question
- **Upload to YouTube**

The clip goes up with the words exactly as they are in the boxes, so edit
first if you want to. A progress bar follows the upload even if you close the
panel. A dropped connection picks up where it stopped instead of starting over.
When it lands, the clip shows **Open in Studio** and **Watch**.

## Safe for your channel

- **It's YouTube's official upload API.** There's no bot clicking around
  YouTube Studio, which would break YouTube's terms and could get a channel
  flagged.
- **ClipMint never sees your password.** You sign in to Google, not to the app.
- **It can upload, and that's all.** It can't delete videos, change your
  channel, or read anything beyond your channel's name.
- **One clip, one click.** Nothing is ever uploaded on its own.
- **Your sign-in stays on your computer.** ClipMint has no servers.
  **Disconnect** hands the permission back to Google and deletes what the app
  stored.
- Every privacy detail is in the new
  [privacy policy](https://klez799.github.io/clipmint/privacy.html).

## Two things to know

- **Google may show "Google hasn't verified this app"** when you connect. Click
  **Advanced → Go to ClipMint**. ClipMint is waiting on Google's review, and the
  warning goes away once that's done.
- **YouTube can keep an upload private** while that review is pending. It
  doesn't always do this, but if it does, ClipMint tells you straight away and
  you can make the video public in Studio with one click.

Only upload videos you have the rights to. A clip of someone else's stream can
get a copyright strike.

## Smaller things

- The sidebar and the "Is this safe?" page no longer say nothing is ever
  uploaded. Now they say when something is: only when you press Upload.
- ClipMint has a home page and privacy policy at
  [klez799.github.io/clipmint](https://klez799.github.io/clipmint/).

---

**ClipMint.exe**: about 220 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**ClipMint-linux-x86_64**: about 278 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc 2.35 or
newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop distribution.**
Every published build is started by the release workflow and asked for its
interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet made a Short with it
on Fedora or Ubuntu. Take `ClipMint-linux-README.txt` too.

**ClipMint-macOS-arm64.zip**: about 165 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still a beta,
and still has never been run on a Mac.** I don't own one, so it is built and
checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a person opening it.
That includes connecting YouTube, which has only been tested on Windows. If it
doesn't start, or signing in doesn't behave as described, that is a bug here:
`~/Movies/ClipMint/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/clipmint/issues/new) are how it gets
fixed.

Also published, for installs from before the rename only: `StreamToShorts.exe`,
`StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64` and `StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip`, the same
three files under their old names. A fresh download wants the ClipMint ones.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
