Type what you want in your own words, and ClipMint understands it.

## Ask for it like a message

The **What to make** box used to understand a fixed list of phrases. Now your AI
model reads the whole thing, the way you'd text a friend who edits for you:

> give me 2 funny ones and 2 where I ask chat a question, around 30 sec each, 720p is fine

Spelling mistakes, Hinglish, several requests in one sentence: all fine. It
works out every setting your words imply, including how many clips, how long,
what kind of video, quality, framing, captions and the edit. Then it answers
right under the box:

> ✦ I'll make 4 clips: 2 funny ones and 2 where you ask chat a question, about 30 seconds each, in 720p.

- **Mixed requests really are mixed.** Ask for 2 funny and 2 that ask a
  question, and you get 2 of each. Before, it took the four best moments,
  which could all be funny. If the video doesn't have enough of one kind, the
  best of the rest fill in, so you never get fewer clips than you asked for.
- **Quality from your words.** "720p is fine" or "best quality" sets
  **Source quality** under Render, tagged *set by your words*.
- **Anything the Edit box can do**, you can say too: "yellow captions at the
  top in bebas, no emoji".
- **Short phrases still work instantly**, even with no AI key. The model
  reads your message about a second after you stop typing. If it's slow, the
  preview says it's still reading and catches up.

## Your data

What you type in the box is sent to your AI provider (Gemini, Groq or
OpenAI) with your own key, so it can be understood. It was already used to
pick moments; the privacy page now says so.

---
**ClipMint.exe**: about 224 MB, Windows, self-contained. Unsigned, so
SmartScreen will warn: **More info → Run anyway**.

**ClipMint-linux-x86_64**: about 280 MB, x86-64, self-contained, glibc 2.35 or
newer. `chmod +x` it and run it. **Still untested on a desktop distribution.**
Every published build is started by the release workflow and asked for its
interface before the release exists, but nobody has yet made a Short with it
on Fedora or Ubuntu. Take `ClipMint-linux-README.txt` too.

**ClipMint-macOS-arm64.zip**: about 167 MB, Apple Silicon only. **Still a beta,
and still has never been run on a Mac.** I don't own one, so it is built and
checked by GitHub's macOS runners and published without a person opening it.
Uploading to YouTube has only been tested on Windows. If it doesn't start, or
signing in doesn't behave as described, that is a bug here:
`~/Movies/ClipMint/app.log` and
[an issue](https://github.com/kleZ799/clipmint/issues/new) are how it gets
fixed.

Also published, for installs from before the rename only: `StreamToShorts.exe`,
`StreamToShorts-linux-x86_64` and `StreamToShorts-macOS-arm64.zip`, the same
three files under their old names. A fresh download wants the ClipMint ones.

On v1.5.0 or later on Windows, or on any Linux build? The app will offer this
to you.
